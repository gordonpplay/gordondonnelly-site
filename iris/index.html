The day-90 test

84 meetings booked in month three. Months four to twelve then have to run near 134 a month to land the 200 logos, so month three is roughly 60% of the rate the rest of the year has to sustain. That step up comes from the first SDR, paid at full weight, and the benchmark report compounding. Under 60 and the plan is not working, and I would say so rather than wait for the quarter to end.

Stated assumptions

Meeting to demo held 75%. Demo to closed won 20%. 70% of booked meetings qualify. These are assumptions, not measurements. Replacing them with twelve months of your HubSpot data is the first thing I do.

Where I have done this. At Knock2 I rebuilt HubSpot attribution so we could actually see which channels were contributing to pipeline, then ran outbound and paid against the same accounts in the same week. MRR rose 36% in the first 60 days.

Vertical is a creative layer, not a targeting one. Half to seventy percent of your customers sit in beauty, wellness and supplements, so that is where the proof and the reference logos are, and where I would start. Why trigger 4 lands in 2026 and would not have in 2023. Meta’s average price per ad fell 16% year over year in Q2 2023. It rose 12% in both Q1 and Q2 2026, and impressions grew 14% to 19% in those same quarters, so buying more reach no longer holds the cost down. Amazon Sponsored Products costs rose 48% between Q1 2019 and Q1 2025 while Facebook’s fell 24%, which makes the marketplace the expensive channel now. And retail media is reallocation, not new budget: Target Roundel budgets doubled in 2026 against 2025 among surveyed marketers. All of it lands on contribution margin, the one number these brands cannot see. The Meta and Amazon figures are from their own earnings releases.
The ceiling nobody has named. US consumer brands at $5M–$150M on Shopify plus Amazon is maybe 3,000 to 4,000 companies, roughly 12,000 reachable people. Touching 5,000 net-new contacts a month exhausts the market in under three months and burns it. The real model is ~2,900 a month, many of them re-engagements on a new signal. That caps outbound near 90 to 120 logos a year regardless of budget, which is exactly why content and partnerships have to carry what they carry.
Where I have done this. At Knock2 I built the outbound program from zero: defined the ICP, then sequenced off website visitors enriched through our own pixel, with BuiltWith and Clay for stack signals. MRR rose 36% in the first 60 days.

Thought leader ads do not drive a click, and that is the point. They buy familiarity before the outbound email lands, follower growth on Drew's profile, and a warm ICP-matched audience that campaign three then converts. Measured on cost per engagement and audience built, not CPL. Everything else is measured on cost per qualified meeting, target under $600, not cost per lead, because a lead that never becomes a meeting is not a lead.
On newsletter and podcast sponsorships, which the role description names: I looked, and I would not buy any. There is no channel that reaches consumer-brand finance leaders. The CPG media has the right companies and the wrong function. Startup CPG, DTC Newsletter, 2PM, Operators all publish founder-heavy audiences and not one of them publishes a finance share. The finance media has the right function and the wrong companies. CFO Dive is 100% of the Fortune 100, and a 26-podcast FP&A roundup contains zero consumer shows. Pavilion has no finance group at all. At $5M–$150M the founder usually is the finance buyer, so the founder channels are the better buy despite being wrong on paper. But convening the missing room costs less than renting someone else's, which is why the dinner budget is real and the sponsorship budget is zero.

At Coologics I wrote and tested creative at volume inside a regulated category and ran the budget against cost per qualified participant rather than cost per lead. Enrollment rose 11x and more than $500,000 of projected study cost was avoided.
