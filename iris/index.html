Why trigger 4 lands in 2026 and would not have in 2023. Meta’s average price per ad fell 16% year over year in Q2 2023. It rose 12% in both Q1 and Q2 2026, and impressions grew 14% to 19% in those same quarters, so buying more reach no longer holds the cost down. Amazon Sponsored Products costs rose 48% between Q1 2019 and Q1 2025 while Facebook’s fell 24%, which makes the marketplace the expensive channel now. And retail media is reallocation, not new budget: Target Roundel budgets doubled in 2026 against 2025 among surveyed marketers. All of it lands on contribution margin, the one number these brands cannot see. The Meta and Amazon figures are from their own earnings releases.
The ceiling nobody has named. US consumer brands at $5M–$150M on Shopify plus Amazon is maybe 3,000 to 4,000 companies, roughly 12,000 reachable people. Touching 5,000 net-new contacts a month exhausts the market in under three months and burns it. The real model is ~2,900 a month, many of them re-engagements on a new signal. That caps outbound near 90 to 120 logos a year regardless of budget, which is exactly why content and partnerships have to carry what they carry.

Thought leader ads do not drive a click, and that is the point. They buy familiarity before the outbound email lands, follower growth on Drew's profile, and a warm ICP-matched audience that campaign three then converts. Measured on cost per engagement and audience built, not CPL. Everything else is measured on cost per qualified meeting, target under $600, not cost per lead, because a lead that never becomes a meeting is not a lead.
Payback does not degrade when this scales, which is the whole argument for the budget. At the Q1 numbers, paid runs a CAC near $5,600 and pays back in roughly two months on a $30k ACV. At four times the spend, $30k a month instead of $7.3k, CAC and payback are identical, because the economics are linear for as long as cost per qualified meeting holds. So the ask is not more budget on faith. Q1 establishes the real cost per qualified meeting, and if it lands under $1,000 we scale; above $1,500 paid does not scale and the gap gets closed by ACV and demo conversion instead.
On newsletter and podcast sponsorships, which the role description names: I looked, and I would not buy any. There is no channel that reaches consumer-brand finance leaders. The CPG media has the right companies and the wrong function. Startup CPG, DTC Newsletter, 2PM, Operators all publish founder-heavy audiences and not one of them publishes a finance share. The finance media has the right function and the wrong companies. CFO Dive is 100% of the Fortune 100, and a 26-podcast FP&A roundup contains zero consumer shows. Pavilion has no finance group at all. At $5M–$150M the founder usually is the finance buyer, so the founder channels are the better buy despite being wrong on paper. But convening the missing room costs less than renting someone else's, which is why the dinner budget is real and the sponsorship budget is zero.

Appendix · Outbound in detail

Outbound, warm first

Cold-first would fail here, and the benchmarks say so. Average B2B cold reply is now 1.4%, down from 3.5% in 2018, and SaaS sits at the bottom of every industry table at under 2%. Positive replies land at 0.3% to 0.6%. Warm outbound, meaning anyone with a prior touchpoint, runs 15% to 22%, and LinkedIn after three or more touches reaches 30% to 40%. With a 14,588-company market, a cold blitz burns the list for a 0.4% return. So the lanes are sized in that order.
