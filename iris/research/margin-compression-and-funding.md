# Margin compression, the DTC-to-wholesale shift, and the funding environment
Research pack, 2026-10-13. Sections 4 and 5 of the WHY NOW report.

Method note: Motley Fool, Seeking Alpha, Insider Monkey, PitchBook and Carta were blocked, so
this leans on SEC 10-Q MD&A and official earnings releases. Stronger evidence than transcripts,
less conversational. Every executive quote below is from a press release or a filing, not a call.

## 4a. The framing finding, and the strongest argument in the whole report

Nearly every US consumer brand's reported 2026 gross margin is inflated by one-time IEEPA
tariff refunds. Headline margin expansion in 2026 is a cash-back artifact, not operating
improvement. Strip the refund and 2026 margin is flat to down. All MEASURED, from filings and
official releases.

| Brand | Headline GM move | How much was the refund | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| FIGS | +820bps to 75.2% | 780bps, $15.4M | 10-Q: new Section 301 duties of 10-12.5% effective 24 Jul 2026 will "continue to negatively impact gross margin for 2026" |
| YETI | +890bps to 66.7% | 780bps tariff, 110bps operational, $45.6M | Adjusted GM +170bps (110 operational, 60 tariff). Separate 110bps headwind from higher YoY tariff costs. 2H 2026 assumes US rates return to about 20% |
| e.l.f. Beauty | 69% to 83%, about +1,400bps | 10-Q attributes "approximately 1,050 basis points benefit from the IEEPA tariff refunds"; remaining ~350bps to "pricing and lower year-over-year tariff rates". $51.1M | |
| Warby Parker | 53.0% to 57.9% (+490bps) | $11.8M, equal to 500bps | The refund exceeded the entire improvement. FY26 guidance embeds $14.4M of refund benefit |
| Levi Strauss | +450bps to 66.2% | 370bps, about $80M in Q3 | Net income still fell about 23% to about $169M (Retail Dive, 8 Oct 2026) |
| Honest Company | GM 48.4%, +800bps headline | Underlying adjusted GM 43.8%, +340bps. H1 refunds $11.9M | The underlying number is a third of the headline gain |

**The line: no one gets a second refund. Six filings say the same thing.**

Levi's CFO Harmit Singh on what the windfall funds: "Our view is, the tariff refunds were
timely." Refunds are going to "incremental marketing to drive demand, enhanced supply chain
capabilities to improve competitiveness over time and sharper value for consumers during key
holiday promotional moments." Consumed by demand generation and holiday discounting, not banked.

Justin Maderia, co-founder, LobsterBoys, on his own refund: "We're just filling back up a
reserve that got drained."

Private brands claimed too (CAPE portal opened 20 Apr 2026, 8am ET; as of 9 Apr 2026 CBP data
showed $127B claimed by 56,000+ importers, potential government liability up to $175B including
interest, 330,000+ importers across 53 million shipments affected). Individual requests:
Learning Resources $10M, Made In $5M, Wild Rye $250,000, Dame about $100,000, Baby's Brew
$50,000+, LobsterBoys about $15,000.
- Rick Woldenberg, CEO, Learning Resources: "It's like getting Taylor Swift tickets." And:
  "They didn't have to do it this way."

## 4b. Margin collapse where there was no refund to hide behind

**VITAL FARMS**, the most vivid datapoint in the pack. MEASURED, Q2 2026 release, 6 Aug 2026:
- Gross margin 38.9% (Q2 2025) to 6.6% (Q2 2026). Minus 3,230 basis points in one year.
- Retail egg prices across the total shell-egg category fell more than 35% YoY.
- $19.5M of losses from dumping excess inventory into breaker and WHOLESALE channels at
  depressed prices. The wholesale channel used as a margin-destroying relief valve.
- FY26 guide held at $775M-$800M revenue but adj EBITDA $0M-$10M on about $790M of revenue.
- CEO Russell Diez-Canseco: "Our second quarter results reflect the industry-wide oversupply and
  price gap pressure we described on our first quarter call." And: "Even as retail egg prices
  across the total shell egg category fell more than 35% year-over-year amid historic
  industry-wide oversupply, we grew our dollar share." Grew retail dollar share over 200bps with
  geography-by-geography price cuts. Share bought with margin.

**CELSIUS**, promotional pressure and DSD billbacks named explicitly. MEASURED, Q2 2026:
- GM 51.5% to 48.1%, minus 340bps. H1 48.2% vs 51.8%.
- Attributed "primarily driven by higher promotional and incentive activity as a percentage of
  revenue and channel mix" and "higher content of DSD versus direct sales, which results in
  higher trade investments and billbacks."
- Revenue +10.6% to $817.9M but net income $55.3M, roughly half the prior quarter's $110.1M.
  Profit halved sequentially while revenue grew.

**ODDITY**, the pure-play online DTC model cracking in real time. MEASURED:
- GM 72.3% to 68.7%, minus 360bps. Revenue $181M, minus 25% YoY. FY2026 guide about 19% revenue
  decline, against FY2025 of $810M and +25%. A roughly 44-point swing in growth rate in a year.
- Cause named in the release: an IL MAKIAGE advertising-algorithm "account dislocation" at its
  largest advertising partner. Acquisition-channel dependency risk in its purest form.
- CEO Oran Holtzman: "We remain hopeful that IL MAKIAGE is on track to achieve normalization and
  we continue to work in close partnership with our largest advertising partner to solve the
  technical issue."

**GROVE COLLABORATIVE**, the end state. MEASURED:
- Revenue $36.6M, minus 16.9%. GM 55.4% to 53.6%. DTC orders 489K, minus 23.6%. DTC active
  customers 509K, minus 23.3%. Revenue per order +6.1% to $69.19. Fewer customers spending more
  is the late-stage DTC signature.
- Cash $8.3M, Siena revolver with $0.4M additional borrowing capacity. Adj EBITDA +$0.5M, third
  consecutive positive quarter. NYSE continued-listing-standards notice 11 Aug 2026.
- Profitability achieved by shrinking is not the same as solvency.

**SOLO BRANDS**, going concern plus the mix flip. MEASURED, 10-Q filed 13 Aug 2026:
- GM 61.3% to 59.9%. 10-Q states "substantial doubt about the Company's ability to continue as a
  going concern."
- Channel mix flipped in twelve months: DTC $59.7M / 64.6% of sales (Q2 2025) to $50.0M / 56.5%
  (Q2 2026). Retail $32.6M / 35.4% to $38.5M / 43.5%. DTC dollars FELL while retail dollars ROSE.
- Inventory cut from $81.6M to $59.6M. At 30 Sep 2025: cash $16.3M against $247.1M of borrowings.
- CEO John Larson: "The third quarter was challenging, reflecting continued pressure on consumer
  demand while we rebuild retail relationships and work through excess retailer inventory
  primarily within our Solo Stove division." And: "current performance underscores the need to
  further accelerate structural cost reductions to align our operating model with demand. SG&A
  was reduced by 35.4% versus the same quarter last year."

**HONEST COMPANY**, profitability-over-growth as a filing: revenue minus 10.9% YoY but ORGANIC
+6.7%. The gap is deliberate exits under "Powering Honest Growth": apparel, the Honest.com
fulfillment channel, and Canada. Q1 2026 was minus 19.7% headline, organic +3.9%. They shrank
the top line 11-20% on purpose, exiting their own fulfillment channel and a country, to buy
340bps of real margin. Cash $105.9M, zero debt.

**OLAPLEX**, the terminal outcome. Acquired by Henkel at $2.06 per share, a 55% premium,
announced 26 Mar 2026, delisting from Nasdaq. Q1 2026 channel divergence: Professional +12.3%,
DTC +13.8%, Specialty Retail minus 13.3%. FY2025 net sales $423.0M, flat, net loss $9.3M.

**ALLBIRDS**, the clearest DTC death-and-pivot of the cycle. Sales $277.47M (2021) to $189.8M
(2024). 30 Mar 2026: asset purchase agreement with American Exchange Group for about $39M.
15 Apr 2026: $50M convertible facility to fund a pivot into AI compute infrastructure.
17 Jun 2026: rebranded Smartbird. The shoe brand sold for $39M; the listed shell raised $50M to
become an AI infra company. It had entered Nordstrom in 2022 as its wholesale answer. It did not
save the company.

**EVERLANE**. Sold to Shein for $100M while carrying $90M of reported debt, roughly a
debt-clearing transaction. L Catterton was shopping it in March 2026. Last equity raise was an
$85M Series F in September 2020. Launched on Amazon in early 2026, the marketplace capitulation
immediately preceding the sale.

## 4c. The channel has already flipped, and private operators say the margin cost out loud

Wholesale now outgrowing DTC at the public comps: Levi's wholesale +6% vs DTC +2% with flat
comps (8 Oct 2026). YETI wholesale +10% vs DTC +7%.

**BOGG**, and this is the why-now in one quote pair. MEASURED company-stated, Modern Retail
1 Jul 2026: $140M 2025 revenue, about 40% from retail partners and 60% DTC plus Amazon, added
about 200 new retail doors in a year, now 7,000+ US locations including Urban Outfitters,
Anthropologie, The Container Store, Tillys.
- Founder Kim Vaccarella: "You love to have DTC, obviously, from a margin perspective" and in
  the same breath "My personal drive is to increase that wholesale number." On why: "they're the
  ones that are talking about us constantly [and] showing their customer Bogg bags on the floor."
- An operator at $140M knows wholesale is margin-dilutive and is accelerating into it anyway,
  because DTC reach no longer delivers.

**HULKEN**, Modern Retail 8 Sep 2026: $100M brand, +50% YoY. Retail revenue now 34% of sales, up
from 16% the prior year. The mix moved 18 points in twelve months. Q4 is about 40% of annual sales.
- CEO Yoni Sheleg on the multichannel inventory squeeze: "We're racing to supply to all these
  channels. And we're making sure that Amazon and Shopify, for our direct-to-consumer, have all
  the inventory they need. And it's quite a challenge, especially while we are expanding our
  assortment at the same time."
- Sheleg accepting dilution: "It's so critical to remember to look at the big picture, because,
  yes, you might squeeze your margins here. But at the end of the day, you're putting more Hulken
  bags in more hands."
- Sheleg on forecasting breaking down: "Because things are moving fast and because we are
  expanding assortment, it's not apples to apples."

**FOLDEN LANE**, the complete model inversion. Now almost 100% wholesale, about 10,000 stores, on
about $3M raised. Co-founder Ben Spivack: "It was really a reinvention of how we thought about
managing and producing inventory to reduce our liability and risk." Only produces inventory
against confirmed retailer POs, and as a wholesale business no longer pays agencies for paid ads
and customer acquisition. Wholesale chosen specifically to kill inventory risk and CAC.

**TRUE CLASSIC**: $500M lifetime, $200M+ 2024 run rate, bootstrapped and profitable, just over 60
FTEs. CEO Ben Yahalom: "Growing wholesale is a huge strategic priority [in 2025]."

**THE COUNTER-CURRENT, and use both sides.** Modern Retail 9 Jul 2024, "Why some brands are being
more cautious about wholesale expansion": brands were then deferring wholesale because "launching
into a new retailer is expensive. It requires brands to produce more inventory, potentially hire
more field reps and do more marketing," and were instead "prioritizing more familiar, low-lift
channels such as Amazon, online marketplaces, or even their own direct-to-consumer sites in order
to extend their runways." An unnamed wellness brand founder: "It's more of a 'not right now'
thinking," because deferring "extends the company's runway for the next six to 12 months and slows
down cash burn."
2024 was "wholesale is too expensive to enter." 2026 is "we're entering anyway because DTC reach
is gone." **The reversal across two years IS the why-now.**

## 4d. Chargebacks, deductions and the cash conversion cycle

THE MAGNITUDE NUMBER, ESTIMATED because it is a vendor claim with no published methodology: SPS
Commerce Revenue Recovery states "Deduction fees typically range from 1%-5% of supplier revenue."
They claim $2B+ recovered for supplier brands, with Owlet recovering $1.4M in six months.
Retailers with dedicated deduction workflows: Walmart, Amazon, Kroger, Target, CVS, Walgreens,
Lowe's, Home Depot, Kohl's, UNFI, KeHE. Note supplypike.com now 301-redirects to SPS Commerce, so
SupplyPike has been absorbed.

On a $50M brand at 3%, that is $1.5M of pure leakage, and it is invisible in the P&L because it
lands as a revenue reduction rather than a cost line. **CITED ON SLIDE 7, TRIGGER 6.**

THE BEST FEE-PAIN QUOTES IN THE CORPUS. Big Spoon Roasters exited 214 Whole Foods locations
effective May 2025 while being a Whole Foods supplier of the year. Modern Retail 28 Jan 2025.
- Co-founder Mark Overbay: "There were several invalid chargeback fees from the distributor that
  we knew we'd have to fight and disprove. We won 100% of those disputes and know how to do that.
  But it's just become so exhausting to do that."
- Overbay on the structure: "The relationship often starts with a big financial hole for the maker
  or supplier, and that can be really difficult to climb out of...when you look at how
  cost-extractive that system is for a supplier like us, the profit margin is very slim."
- Overbay on losing price control: "we tried to have that conversation many times, from the
  distributor to the retail level, and were always given the same answer, that pricing is its own
  team."

- Al Sambar, General Partner, XRC Ventures: "Vendor compliance penalties on top of their payment
  terms can make you wish you never even thought about wholesale if you aren't ready for them."
- Brad Charron, CEO, Aloha: "Before you go into a place like Target or Whole Foods or Wegmans, you
  better be ready to go. Because once you're out, be prepared to sit in purgatory for five to
  seven years." Also: "There was also cash flow mismanagement, and you can't be poorly financed
  entering a big-box retailer." On concentration risk when Target dropped them: "that's half of
  your revenue going to go out the door."
- Genevieve Gilbreath, Founding Partner, Springdale Ventures: "Brands have become hyper-fixated on
  landing at big chain retailers like Whole Foods and Target without the foundation to support
  it," and "without enough proven demand or proper cash reserves, these channels end up becoming
  a liability instead of a growth lever."

THE SAKS CASE. Saks Global announced in Feb 2025 that outstanding vendor balances would be paid in
12 monthly installments beginning Jul 2025. A retailer unilaterally converting trade payables into
a year-long unsecured loan from its suppliers. It then filed Chapter 11 on 14 Jan 2026 with $3.4B
of debt, exiting 26 Jun 2026 as Exemplar Luxury Group. Vendor exposure: Elisamama owed six
figures, last payment August 2025; one brand owed $25,000 by Saks Off 5th; another about $100,000
by Saks Fifth Avenue. CEO Geoffroy van Raemdonck said post-filing that payments for goods provided
after 14 Jan would get "administrative priority payment status in Chapter 11," which says nothing
about pre-petition balances.

## 4e. Forecastability has broken, and that is what brands will pay to fix

Modern Retail, "Brands grapple with strained cash flow amid tariffs," 3 Jul 2025. Flaus paused a
$30,000 celebrity pop-up; Beau Ties shelved a $4,000 equipment purchase; Loftie lamp sales down
80% in May; Flaus moved from 8-10 weeks of inventory to five months stockpiled; Beau Ties ordered
3.5x typical inventory in January and 3x a standard monthly order in May.
- Steve Carpenter, COO North America, Creditsafe: "Tariff uncertainty is forcing many retailers
  and suppliers to front-load inventory ahead of the holidays, tying up significant cash in goods
  that may not move immediately."
- Samantha Coxe, Founder and CEO, Flaus: "We have less cash to do experiments with because it's
  all tied up into our inventory."
- Matthew Hassett, CEO, Loftie: "If our sales are down 80%, we don't have that cash coming in to

- Greg Shugar, Beau Ties of Vermont: "I'm afraid to jump into anything right now. Everyone is just
  on eggshells."

RAW MATERIALS, Modern Retail 31 Aug 2026. Vinyl prices +1.8% (2024), +2.9% (2025), +5.9% (2026).
PVC hit record highs in April 2026. And the forecastability problem quantified at scale: Reynolds
Consumer Products revised its 2026 incremental commodity cost estimate from $200M in April to

- Andrew Witkin, Founder and CEO, StickerYou: "It certainly has affected everything. Input costs
  are input costs."

## 5c. Debt and inventory financing: the rate card, and Ampla resolved

MEASURED, lender-stated APRs reported by Modern Retail 28 May 2024:
- Traditional banks: 7%-9%, hardest to qualify for
- Dwight Funding (asset-based): 9%-15%, prefers $5M-$10M+ revenue, $4B+ funded since inception,
  about 100 companies in portfolio, clients include Dagne Dover, Chubbies, Rhone
- Venture-backed fintech lenders: 15%-25%. The Ampla/Clearco/Wayflyer tier.
Also active: Rosenthal & Rosenthal, SG Credit Partners.
- Ben Brachot, Dwight Funding: "I think the pendulum is swinging back to... a more healthy mix of
  equity and credit."

THE ARITHMETIC OF THE SQUEEZE, the cleanest statement of the business case: a brand locked out of
bank debt and out of equity below $10M revenue is borrowing inventory money at 15-25%, while
carrying extended retailer receivables and 1-5% deduction leakage.

**AMPLA, RESOLVED:**
- 14 May 2024: Ampla reportedly considering a sale; Snaxshot spoke with brands that "had their
  lines of credit cut" by Ampla. By 28 May 2024 Modern Retail described it as "facing funding
  challenges, seeking buyer, cutting credit lines."
- OUTCOME, verified by primary source: getampla.com now 301-redirects to
  fundthrough.com/ampla-retail-factoring/, which states "FundThrough has acquired Ampla,
  strengthening its digital-first invoice funding solution for retail and consumer brands." The
  page also states Ampla "surpassed $2B in loan originations and handled $5T in transaction volume
  through its platform."
- The acquisition DATE could not be confirmed. Unconfirmed.
- WHY IT MATTERS: the DTC-native fintech lender that cut brands' credit lines mid-cycle was
  absorbed, and the product that replaced it is INVOICE FACTORING, which only works if you already
  have a receivable from a creditworthy retailer. The financing market has reorganised itself
  around wholesale receivables. Both a cause and a consequence of the DTC-to-wholesale shift.

NOT FINDABLE: nothing reachable on Settle, Parker, Wayflyer, 8fig, Assembled Brands, Clearco or

## Corrections to the earlier Amazon-fees section

1. **Amazon PAUSED the advertising payment change.** The earlier report described the 15 Apr 2026
   ad-deduction change as in force. Sellers organised a one-day ad boycott on 9 Apr 2026 and
   Amazon paused the advertising payment change on 14 Apr 2026. Eugene Khayman, co-founder,
   Million Dollar Sellers: "It was ultimately to prove a point that seller frustration is at an
   all-time high." The payout-timing change is separate and did proceed: a new "Deliver Date +
   7 days" structure delaying seller access to cash by about 10-15 days or more.
   (The deck never asserted this, so no deck correction was needed.)
2. **Amazon seller base is consolidating**, which strengthens the fee argument. Marketplace Pulse
   via Modern Retail 23 Apr 2026: active sellers fell from 584,000 (Jan 2025) to 500,000
   (Mar 2026). New seller registrations in 2025 were 165,000, the lowest since 2015 and minus 44%
   YoY. Fewer than 8,000 sellers now account for about half of US third-party GMV, down from about
   15,000. About $80 of a $150 retail item goes to Amazon across commissions, fulfillment, storage
   and advertising. Ben Donovan, Insights Lead, Marketplace Pulse: "It's become a much harder
   environment. Selling on Amazon is far less of a 'side project, stay-at-home mom lists some
   products' kind of business."

# Supplement: Amazon fee mechanics and advertising load (2026-10-13)

Second pass of the hard-economics research. No previously reported figure changed; the following
is new material.

## The corridor problem. The best FP&A argument in the research, and it is now on the deck.

Nova Analytics' framing. The aged-inventory penalty threshold tightened to **181 days** (from 365)
while the low-inventory-level penalty bites **below 28 days of cover**. The optimal inventory band
narrowed from both ends in the same year. Compounding it, the low-inventory-level fee has been
measured **per FNSKU since 2026-01-15** (previously per parent ASIN), which materially increases
exposure for any brand with variants.

A brand must now land inside a window it cannot see without per-SKU, per-FNSKU days-of-cover
forecasting. **That states a product requirement as an external constraint rather than a benefit
claim**, which is why it replaced the take-rate line as trigger 4's opener on the deck.

Apparel, footwear, bags, jewelry and watches are excluded from the 181-270 day bands.

## Amazon storage fees. A resolved conflict and a live event.

- Current off-peak standard rate is **$0.87/cu ft, not $0.78**. Nova Analytics (2026-07-03, citing
  a Seller Central schedule revision dated 2026-05-29) reports a mid-year increase effective
  2026-07-01; SmartScout's historical card confirms $0.78 was the 2024/H1-2026 rate. Publish $0.87
  as current and footnote $0.78.
- DO NOT PUBLISH the "$0.76 / $2.10" prior-rate figures from that same Nova piece. They reconcile
  with nothing else.
- DERIVED headline: standard-size storage goes from **$0.87 to $2.40/cu ft on 1 October**, a 2.8x
  step-change in the quarter a brand holds the most inventory (oversize $0.56 to $1.40). All
  sources agree on the peak rates. As of today this is live, not forthcoming.
- The aged-inventory surcharge threshold tightened from 365 to 181 days, and the 12-15 month
  per-unit rate doubled in 2026 from $0.15 to $0.30.
- Assessment is a snapshot on the **15th of each month**, so a removal order submitted by the 14th
  avoids that month's charge. Tactically useful for outreach timing.

## Amazon take rate. Use this formulation.

Fee-only take is **27-38% of revenue** for a standard-size FBA SKU (three independent sources
converge). Approximately **30% measured against 3P GMV** from Amazon's audited filings.
**39-59% all-in** once advertising is loaded, and the all-in figure is driven **by price point
more than by category**. That last clause is the useful part and it is the opposite of how most
people talk about this.

The deck previously said "about 38% once FBA, referral fees, returns and ads net out," which
conflated the top of the fee-only range with the all-in figure. **Corrected on the deck.**

Standing guidance: the widely-quoted "over 50%" is Marketplace Pulse's undisclosed-sample P&L
estimate from 2023, not a 2026 measurement.

On the 2026 fee increase, three arguably-true numbers spanning 0.5% to 10%: Amazon's own $0.08
headline, Amazon's own $0.51 price-band detail, and amzprep (a 3PL, so an interested party,
ESTIMATED) saying "most sellers are paying 8-10% more, not 0.5%". That spread is itself a pitch.
